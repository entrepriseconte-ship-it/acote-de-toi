Château-Dauphin

Medieval castle in Pontgibaud in the Puy-de-Dôme département of France · Pontgibaud, France

Photo: Groumfy69, Public domain

About

Château-Dauphin is a medieval castle in the commune of Pontgibaud in the Puy-de-Dôme département of France.

History

The castle owes its name to the coat of arms of the person who built it in the 12th century: Robert I, Count of Auvergne, on whose arms was a dolphin (dauphin). The original construction was modified in the 15th century by Gilbert III Motier de La Fayette, who strengthened the defences and enlarged the keep. Abandoned in the 17th century for a more comfortable building, and damaged during the French Revolution, the castle was finally restored in the 19th century by Count César III of Pontgibaud. The castle has been occupied since 1756 by the family of a King's musketeer, César I de Moré. The present owners, the Count and Countess Gabriel de Germiny, are his descendants. The castle, garden and museum are open to the public.
