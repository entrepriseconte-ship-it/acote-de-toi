Château de Dampierre

Château · Dampierre-en-Yvelines, France

Photo: Rémih, CC BY-SA 4.0

About

The Château de Dampierre is a French Baroque château in Dampierre-en-Yvelines, in the Vallée de Chevreuse, approximately 35 kilometres southwest of Paris. Built between 1675 and 1683 by Jules Hardouin-Mansart for the Duke of Chevreuse, it is surrounded by an extensive park with formal gardens traditionally attributed to André Le Nôtre.

History

The château was rebuilt between 1675 and 1683 by Jules Hardouin-Mansart for the Duke of Chevreuse, son-in-law of Jean-Baptiste Colbert. It is an example of late seventeenth-century French Baroque architecture. In the nineteenth century, Honoré Théodoric d'Albert de Luynes, an archaeologist and art collector, commissioned architect Félix Duban to renovate the château. The works included the refurbishment of its interiors and the decoration of the Grande Galerie, involving artists such as Pierre-Charles Simart and Jean-Auguste-Dominique Ingres. After more than 350 years in the possession of the Luynes family, the château was acquired in 2018 by French businessman Franky Mulliez, founder of Kiloutou, who initiated a major restoration programme.
