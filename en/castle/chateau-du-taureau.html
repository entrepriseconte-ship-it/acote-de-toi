Château du Taureau

Island in Finistère, France · Plouezoc'h, France

Photo: Bulo78, Public domain

About

The Château du Taureau is a sea fort in the Bay of Morlaix, off Plouezoc'h in Finistère, Brittany, France. The town of Morlaix began building it in the 1540s to defend the approaches to its port following an English raid in 1522. Initially maintained and commanded by the municipality, the fort passed into royal control in the 17th century. As part of the strengthening of France's coastal defences under Louis XIV, Vauban drew up plans for its reconstruction in 1689; the works were completed in 1745.

History

In the early 16th century Morlaix was a centre of the Breton linen trade, but its position at the head of a long estuary left it exposed to attack from the sea. An English force sacked the town in 1522. Coastal watches and temporary batteries were organized afterwards, requiring men and artillery to be sent from Morlaix and the neighbouring parishes to guard the approaches to the bay. In 1542 Jean IV de Brosse, duke of Étampes, inspected these defences. Ambroise Masson, a notable of Morlaix, proposed replacing the costly watches with a permanent fort on the Taureau rock, near the entrance to the estuary. The duke supported the proposal, and the town raised contributions to begin construction. By 1544 the fort could accommodate a garrison, and Morlaix appointed Jean de Kermellec as its first captain. Unlike a royal fortress, the Taureau was financed and administered by the town that it protected. The first works included an artillery tower, subsequently known as the Tour Française, with guns covering the channel. Construction and repairs continued throughout the 16th century. The tower collapsed in 1609 and was rebuilt in 1614, a date recorded on a surviving stone inscription. In 1564 Morlaix secured an arrangement under which a departing municipal procureur could serve as captain in the following year. The fort's command thus became part of the town's civic administration, an arrangement that also made it the object of repeated disputes over authority and revenue. Troilus de Mesgouez, governor of Morlaix, attempted to claim control of the fort in the 1560s, but the town successfully defended its privileges. A more prolonged confrontation followed during the French Wars of Religion.
